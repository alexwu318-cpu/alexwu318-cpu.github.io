NASA · Jet Propulsion Laboratory

The largest planetary spacecraft NASA has built is headed for an ice-covered ocean.

Europa Clipper launched October 14, 2024, on a six-year journey to Jupiter's moon Europa — a world with a global saltwater ocean sealed under miles of ice. Its job isn't to find life directly, but to find out whether Europa could support it.

Mission Timeline

A six-year, 1.8-billion-mile route

Clipper uses Mars and Earth flybys to gain speed on its way out, saving fuel over a direct route. The Earth flyby in December 2026 is the last gravity assist before the long coast to Jupiter.

Size

NASA's biggest planetary spacecraft

With solar arrays and antennas fully extended, Clipper stretches roughly 30.5 meters long and 17.6 meters wide — larger than a basketball court, and the biggest spacecraft NASA has built for a planetary mission.

Instruments

Radar that sees through ice

Clipper's REASON instrument sends radar beneath Europa's icy shell, revealing pockets of water, cracks and structure invisible from the surface — one of nine instruments working together on every flyby.

Why Europa

A moon worth six years of travel

A Hidden Ocean

More water than every ocean on Earth

Fun Fact Clipper will fly by Europa about 50 times instead of orbiting it directly, to limit exposure to Jupiter's radiation.
Strong evidence points to a global saltwater ocean beneath Europa's icy shell—one that may hold more liquid water than all of Earth's oceans combined. Clipper's three science objectives are to measure the ice shell's thickness, study its interaction with the ocean below, and characterize Europa's composition and geology.
Not a Life-Detector

Checking if life is even possible

Fun Fact Europa Clipper won't look for life directly— its job is to determine whether Europa could support it at all.
Rather than searching for life itself, Europa Clipper studies whether Europa has the right ingredients: liquid water, chemistry and an energy source. That information will help scientists evaluate the habitability of icy moons throughout the Solar System.
